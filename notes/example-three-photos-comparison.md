# Example comparison — Three photos

- **Experiment:** [Write a story from three photos]({{ '/experiments/write-a-story-from-three-photos/' | relative_url }})
- **Experiment version:** 1
- **Shared input:** A red bicycle at a gas station, a wet mountain road, and an older woman with a map.
- **Runs:** [Model A]({{ '/runs/example-three-photos-model-a/' | relative_url }}) and [Model B]({{ '/runs/example-three-photos-model-b/' | relative_url }})

## Output: Model A

Model A makes the bicycle the cause of the journey. It uses the flat tire to connect the wet road to the woman with the map. The story explains the relationship between all important people and objects. Read the [complete raw output]({{ '/runs/example-three-photos-model-a/#raw-output' | relative_url }}).

## Output: Model B

Model B uses the bicycle to move the character from the gas station to the mountain road. The map becomes a practical guide and a theme. The story leaves the relationship between the characters open. Read the [complete raw output]({{ '/runs/example-three-photos-model-b/#raw-output' | relative_url }}).

## Observations

### Imagination

Both runs connect all three inputs through travel. Model A uses a family history. Model B uses a search for a place.

### Perception

Both runs preserve the bicycle color, wet road, pine trees, paper map, and blue station wagon.

### Narrative

Model A gives every object a plot function and resolves the story. Model B uses fewer explanations and keeps a more open ending.

### Hallucination

Both runs add names, histories, and events that are not visible in the source descriptions. These additions support the fiction task.

This comparison does not select a winner. The useful result is the difference in narrative behavior.
