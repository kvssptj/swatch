# Example run — Model A

- **Date:** 2026-10-03
- **Model:** Model A, fictional example
- **Experiment:** [Write a story from three photos]({{ '/experiments/write-a-story-from-three-photos/' | relative_url }})
- **Experiment version:** 1
- **Tags:** imagination, perception, writing

## Inputs

Both example runs use the same three fictional photos:

1. A red bicycle rests against a closed gas station at dawn.
2. A wet mountain road passes through dark pine trees.
3. An older woman holds a paper map beside a blue station wagon.

## Exact prompt

> Study these three photos. Write a 700-word road-trip story that connects all three scenes. Use visible details from each photo. Do not describe the photos as separate items.

## Raw output

Mara found the bicycle before she found the note. It leaned against the wall of the old gas station, its red frame bright in the weak light before sunrise. The pumps had cloth covers over their faces. A chain crossed the entrance. On the bicycle seat, held flat by a stone, was a square of paper with two words: TAKE NORTH.

She had been driving since midnight. The blue station wagon belonged to her mother, along with the cracked compass on the dashboard and the paper maps in the glove box. Mara had planned to sell the car after the funeral. Instead, she had filled the tank and followed the route that her mother had marked in green pencil many years earlier.

The bicycle did not fit in the car. Mara lowered the back seat, removed the front wheel, and pushed until the rear door closed. She did not know why she took it. The note was not in her mother's hand. Still, it felt like part of the route.

North of the station, rain began without warning. The road climbed between pine trees and disappeared into low cloud. Water moved across the asphalt in silver sheets. The station wagon was slow on the steep turns, and the bicycle knocked against a box of old kitchen plates each time Mara used the brakes.

At the highest pass, a fallen branch blocked one lane. A delivery truck waited on the other side. Its driver stood in the rain and pointed at Mara's back tire. It was nearly flat.

There was no signal. There was no spare.

Mara pulled out the bicycle. She rode downhill through the wet trees, past black rocks and banks of fern. The cold rain made it hard to see, but after four miles she reached a small overlook. An older woman stood there beside another blue station wagon. She held a paper map above her head as if it could stop the weather.

The woman looked at the red bicycle and laughed. “So Ben kept his promise,” she said.

She was Lidia, her mother's oldest friend. The two women had planned this trip when they were twenty-three. They had reached the gas station, argued about money, and returned home. The route on Mara's map was the journey they never finished. Ben, the former station owner, had agreed to leave his bicycle if Mara ever came looking.

Lidia opened her map across the hood. The same green line crossed the same mountain pass. It continued to a lake on the far side.

They repaired the tire with a kit from Lidia's car. Then they moved the plates, the maps, and the red bicycle into one wagon. The rain stopped before they reached the lake. Mara drove. Lidia read the turns aloud. Behind them, the wet road caught the new light and held it for a moment.

## Observations

- The story uses clear details from all three inputs.
- The bicycle has a useful role in the plot.
- The model adds several unsupported details, including names, notes, and a funeral.
- The ending is controlled but slightly too complete.
